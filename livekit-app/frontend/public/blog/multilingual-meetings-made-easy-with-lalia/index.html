Bring people together without arranging a separate interpreter for every everyday meeting. See how Lalia combines spoken translation, bilingual captions, and clearer follow-ups.

A meeting should start with the people in the room—not a search for someone who can translate between them. When your team, client, or business partner speaks another language, even a simple conversation can turn into a scheduling project.

Lalia makes that everyday connection easier. Live spoken voice translation and bilingual captions let people speak in their own language and follow the conversation in theirs. For routine multilingual meetings, that means you can get people together without first arranging a separate interpreter.

Less coordination before the conversation

Think about a quick project update. You need to review a timeline, ask a supplier a question, or introduce a colleague in another country. The conversation itself might be straightforward. Finding a time that works for everyone—and an interpreter—can take longer than the meeting.

With Lalia, you can call a saved contact, share a guest link, or schedule a group meeting. Each participant chooses their language. Translation becomes part of the meeting experience, rather than another service you need to coordinate before anyone can say hello.

That opens up room for smaller, more spontaneous conversations: a ten-minute check-in, a follow-up question, or an introduction you would otherwise postpone. You can focus on who needs to be involved and what you want to accomplish.

Speak naturally. Follow along in your language.

It is easier to explain an idea when you are not translating every sentence in your head. Lalia translates speech out loud into a participant’s chosen language, with live bilingual captions alongside it. People can listen, read, and respond in the language they feel comfortable using.

Instead of asking a bilingual colleague to spend the whole call relaying messages, that person can take part as a teammate. Instead of reducing your point to the few phrases you know in another language, you can explain the context, ask a follow-up, and keep contributing.

Your voice. Their language. More room for the conversation that matters.

The captions offer another way to follow the discussion. You can refer back to a sentence on screen or ask someone to clarify a detail. Translation helps people connect; clear communication still comes from listening, checking understanding, and giving each other time to respond.

Built around the meetings you already have

You do not need a major international conference to benefit from a simpler way to meet across languages. Everyday conversations are often where the difference matters most.

  • Team check-ins: give colleagues in different countries space to share progress and raise questions in their own language.
  • Client introductions: spend more of the conversation understanding what someone needs, with less time arranging language support beforehand.
  • Supplier and partner updates: talk through a delivery, a proposal, or a next step without making every short discussion a separate coordination exercise.
  • One-on-one follow-ups: call a contact directly when a quick conversation would be clearer than a long chain of translated messages.

These are examples of how you might use Lalia, not a promise that every conversation will be effortless. A quiet space, a reliable connection, and a clear agenda still help. When a name, number, deadline, or decision matters, take a moment to confirm it together.

Keep the clarity after the call

A productive meeting should leave people knowing what happens next. Lalia brings bilingual transcripts and AI meeting insights into the same experience, so you can review what was discussed and generate summaries, action items, or decisions reports.

You can email the takeaways and transcript to participants, contacts, or other recipients. That gives the conversation a useful second life: a shared reference for the person completing a task, the colleague who needs an update, or the team reviewing its next steps.

Review the generated notes before sharing them, especially owners, dates, and important commitments. The goal is a clearer follow-up—not another document everyone has to untangle.

Choose the right support for the conversation

Live translation can reduce the need to arrange an interpreter for many routine conversations. It does not make professional interpretation unnecessary in every situation. Legal proceedings, medical discussions, and other sensitive or highly specialized meetings may still call for a qualified human interpreter.

For everyday collaboration, though, there is real value in being able to bring someone into a conversation more easily. A language difference does not have to become the reason you put off a useful meeting.

Start with a simple hello

Try a short conversation first. Invite a colleague or partner, choose your languages, and ask a question you would normally discuss together. Listen to the translated speech, follow the captions, and see how the experience feels.

You can explore Lalia’s live voice demo or create a free account to get started. Your next meeting can begin with the people you want to talk to—and a little less work getting the conversation started.
