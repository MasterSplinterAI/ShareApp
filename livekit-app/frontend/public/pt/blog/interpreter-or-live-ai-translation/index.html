CHOOSING THE RIGHT SUPPORT

Interpreter or live translation?
Choose by the meeting.

Some conversations need a professional interpreter. Many don’t. Here is a simple way to tell the difference.

Your supplier has a delay to explain. A new colleague has onboarding questions. A client wants a walkthrough on Thursday. Three meetings. Two languages. One question: do we need an interpreter?

The honest answer is that it depends on the meeting, not on the language.

Live translation now lets people speak their own language and follow along in theirs. That makes many conversations easier to hold. It does not make every conversation the same.

Start with one question

Before you book anyone, ask: if one sentence were misunderstood, what would it cost?

Most meetings fall into one of three groups.

  • Easy to correct. A check-in, a status update, an introduction. A mix-up gets fixed in the next message.
  • Worth confirming. Preços, timelines, a change in scope. A mistake costs time or money, but you can catch it.
  • Critical. Legal, medical, safety, or sensitive HR conversations—or any meeting where the rules call for a qualified interpreter.

What each option does best

An interpreter brings judgment. They notice when a word lands wrong. They can clarify, slow down, and adapt to the people in the room.

Live translation brings availability. It is there the moment a call starts. Lalia translates speech out loud and shows bilingual captions, so people can listen and read along in 30+ languages.

Interpreters bring judgment.
Live translation brings availability.

Most teams need both. Just not in the same meetings.

Where live translation fits

Live translation works best for the meetings that happen every week. They are short, frequent, and easy to follow up.

  • Team check-ins: share progress and questions in your own language.
  • Supplier updates: talk through a delivery or a timeline, then confirm it in writing.
  • Client introductions: spend the call on what they need, not on who can translate.
  • Quick follow-ups: call a contact directly when a short conversation beats a long email thread.

These are examples of how you might use Lalia, not customer case studies.

When to book an interpreter

Some meetings deserve a professional. Book a qualified interpreter for legal proceedings, medical conversations, safety briefings, and sensitive personnel discussions. Do the same whenever a regulation or policy requires one.

Live translation can still help around those meetings. Use it for prep calls and follow-ups. Save the interpreter’s time for the moment that needs their judgment.

For the meetings in between

Preços calls and scope changes sit in the middle. A few small habits close most of the gap.

  1. Read along as you listen. Captions give you a second look at names and numbers.
  2. Put key details in writing. Share prices, dates, and product codes in the chat.
  3. Close with a shared summary. If the host turns on transcripts, Lalia can create a summary and action items. Review it, then email it to everyone.
  4. Change the plan if the meeting changes. If a check-in turns into a negotiation, pause and reschedule with the right support.

Start with your next routine call

You do not need a new policy to try this. Pick one everyday meeting. Invite the people who need to talk. Choose your languages and see how it feels.
