A client has a question. A supplier has an update. Your team needs to make a decision. The meeting should be simple. Then you realize not everyone speaks the same language.

For many everyday conversations, that adds another task: find an interpreter, check availability, share the brief, and fit one more person into the schedule. Lalia offers an easier way to bring people together. Live voice translation and bilingual captions help participants follow the same meeting in their own languages.

You can focus on the discussion without arranging a separate interpreter for every routine call. Here is what that looks like before, during, and after a meeting.

Less to arrange before you meet

Planning a multilingual meeting can involve more than finding a time that works. Someone has to think about how the conversation will be translated and how everyone will follow along.

With Lalia, translation is part of the meeting experience. You can call a saved contact, share a guest link, or schedule a group meeting. Each participant chooses their language. That puts fewer moving parts between the people who need to talk.

Think about a weekly project update. The same team meets often, the topics change quickly, and questions come up on the spot. Having live translation available in the room makes it easier to hold that conversation when the work needs it.

Speak naturally. Hear the translation.

Lalia combines spoken voice translation with live bilingual captions. You speak in your language. Other participants hear translated speech in their chosen language and can read the captions alongside the conversation.

That gives people two ways to follow along. They can listen to the discussion and look at the text when they want to check a phrase. Participants do not all have to switch to one shared language to take part.

Your voice. Their language.

Lalia supports live captions and translation in 30+ languages. Before the meeting, check the available language options and try a short call with the people who will be joining. A quick test helps everyone get comfortable with the audio, captions, and pace.

A simpler way to meet, every day

The benefit becomes clear in the conversations that keep work moving. A client can ask a question in the language they are comfortable using. A partner can explain an idea directly. A teammate can join the discussion without preparing every sentence in a second language.

Client and sales calls

Use a translated meeting to walk through a service, explain a proposal, or answer questions. Instead of asking the client to do all the language work, you can both speak in your own languages and check that the details are understood.

Team updates and project planning

Bring colleagues together to discuss progress, blockers, and next steps. Live translation helps the group stay in one shared conversation while each person follows in their chosen language.

Supplier and partner conversations

Talk through requirements, timelines, or an upcoming launch with people in another market. For example, an English-speaking project lead and a Spanish-speaking partner could review a task together, ask follow-up questions, and confirm the next step. This is an example of how a meeting could work, rather than a customer case study.

Keep the clarity after the call

A useful meeting leaves everyone knowing what happens next. That can be harder when people leave with different notes or remember a translated detail differently.

Lalia provides bilingual transcripts and AI tools that turn the discussion into an executive summary, action items, or a decisions report. You can review the output, then email the report and transcript to participants, saved contacts, or other recipients.

That gives the team a shared place to check what was discussed. Review names, dates, numbers, and task owners before sending the follow-up. It is a small step that helps turn a good conversation into a clear plan.

Set up your first translated meeting

You do not need a complicated rollout to see whether live translation fits your team. Start with one short, familiar conversation.

  1. Choose a useful first call. Pick a routine check-in, introduction, or project update with a clear goal.
  2. Invite the people who need to talk. Start a direct call, share a guest link, or schedule a group meeting in Lalia.
  3. Choose your languages and check the sound. Make sure each person can hear the audio and see the captions before the discussion begins.
  4. Talk, then confirm the next step. Review the transcript and any AI-generated notes. Share the checked summary with the people who need it.

If you want to hear the experience first, create a free account. You can also compare the available plans before choosing the right fit for your meetings.

Small habits for better conversations

Live translation works best as part of a thoughtful conversation. Give each person time to finish. Use a clear microphone. Keep background noise low, and pause between longer points so everyone has room to follow and respond.

When a detail matters, say it plainly and confirm it together. Share product names, reference numbers, or other unfamiliar terms in writing as well. These are useful meeting habits in any language.

The right translation setup depends on the purpose of the meeting. Lalia makes routine multilingual conversations easier; a meeting that requires certified interpretation or specialist language support may still call for a professional interpreter.

For your next team update or client introduction, start with a simpler question: who would you like to talk to if language were less of a barrier?
