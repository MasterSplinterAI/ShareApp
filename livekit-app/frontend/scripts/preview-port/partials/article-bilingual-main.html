Anyone who has held a meeting across two languages knows the pause. Someone finishes a point, the room goes quiet while it is repeated in the second language, and the natural back-and-forth disappears.

When people have to wait twice for every turn, a thirty-minute meeting accomplishes fifteen minutes of work. Quiet teammates hold back questions because interrupting feels twice as expensive.

Designing a room where nobody waits means removing that single-file queue—before the call starts, while people are speaking, and after they hang up.

Why most bilingual calls make people wait

Waiting happens in three places during an everyday cross-language meeting.

  • Before the call: guests are asked to install software, create an account, or wait for someone who can relay between languages.
  • During the call: everyone shares one audio channel, so translation either talks over the speaker or forces the speaker to stop after every sentence.
  • After the call: one side writes notes in their language and the other side waits for a translated summary.

Every listener gets their own language lane

In Lalia, translation is not broadcast into one shared microphone channel. Each target language in the room has its own dedicated audio lane.

When you choose your language on the way in, your browser or app subscribes only to the voice track meant for you, alongside live bilingual captions on screen. A Spanish listener hears Spanish; a Japanese listener hears Japanese; an English listener hears English—all from the same statement, without anyone waiting in line.

Nobody has to pick a single “meeting language.”
Everyone joins in their own.

Joining without the pre-call scramble

A fast room starts before the first word is spoken. Lalia gives you two ways to bring people in without setup delays.

  1. One-tap direct calls. Ring a saved contact directly on their phone or browser when a quick five-minute conversation beats a long email thread.
  2. Browser guest links and QR codes. Share a link or scannable QR code with a client or partner. Guests pick their display name and language in mobile Safari or Chrome—no download and no account required.
  3. Thirty-plus languages ready in the room. Each participant chooses what they speak and hear on the pre-join screen.

When people switch languages mid-call

Real bilingual teams rarely stick to one language from start to finish. Someone greets a client in Spanish, explains a technical detail in English, and answers a side question in French.

Lalia detects the language of each spoken phrase automatically and routes translation only to listeners who need it—skipping translation when the speaker is already using the listener’s language so you never hear your own language echoed back to you.

Leaving with the same record

When the host enables transcript storage for a meeting, Lalia saves the bilingual transcript as the conversation happens. Right after the call, you can generate an executive summary, action items, or a decisions report, review the details, and email it to everyone in one click.

These scenarios are illustrative examples of how Lalia works. For legal, medical, or regulated proceedings that require certified human interpretation, book a qualified professional interpreter.
